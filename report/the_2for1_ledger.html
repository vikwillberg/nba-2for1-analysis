The 2-for-1 Ledger

With about 40 seconds left in a quarter, teams shoot early so they can get the ball back for the last shot. I rebuilt 9,581 of those moments from play-by-play to measure what the move is worth, when it stops working, and which teams leave points on the table.

What I found
+0.2

Net points per opportunity from going 2-for-1, after adjusting for how and when the ball was gained (OLS +0.22, inverse propensity weighting +0.17). The true value in the simulation is +0.215.

36%

How much a raw comparison overstates it. Teams that go 2-for-1 more often start from a steal and with more time on the clock, and both of those help on their own.

0:33

The threshold. Gain the ball with 0:33 or more and the move pays about +0.26. Gain it later and you must shoot within 3 to 5 seconds; that rushed shot eats most of the gain.

−0.20

The cost of shooting too early. A first shot with 0:34+ left raises the opponent's chance of getting two possessions of its own from 19% to 52%.

10.5

Points per season the median team leaves on the table through passed 2-for-1s and early shots. The best-to-worst gap is 7.5 points, about a quarter of a win, for a decision that costs nothing to fix.

Data and pipeline

Rebuilding possessions from raw events

Play-by-play logs events, not possessions. The analysis needs to know exactly when each team gained the ball, how it gained it, and when it first shot, so the SQL layer reconstructs possessions from the event stream before anything is modeled.

  1. PythonSimulate127,163 events from the last 75 seconds of Q1–Q3 in 3,690 games, in the PlayByPlayV3 layout.
  2. SQLStageParse the ISO clock (PT00M34.50S → 34.5) and tag which team has the ball on every event.
  3. SQLPossessionsWindow functions turn events into 59,141 possessions, each dated to the event that handed the ball over.
  4. SQLWindowsKeep the first possession gained at 0:30–0:45 in each quarter: 9,581 opportunities.
  5. RModelEstimate the effect three ways, split it by clock and shot timing, and grade every team.
sql/02_possessions.sql (excerpt)DuckDB SQL
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE possessions AS
WITH ev AS (
    SELECT *,
           LAG(ball_team_id) OVER w AS prev_ball_team
    FROM pbp
    WHERE action_type <> 'period'
    WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY game_id, period ORDER BY action_number)
),
numbered AS (
    SELECT *,
           SUM(CASE WHEN prev_ball_team IS NULL
                      OR ball_team_id <> prev_ball_team THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
               OVER (PARTITION BY game_id, period ORDER BY action_number
                     ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING)                         AS poss_seq
    FROM ev
),
-- ... aggregate first / last event of each possession (agg CTE) ...

    -- when did this team gain the ball?
    CASE WHEN a.first_type = 'Rebound' AND a.first_sub = 'Defensive'
         THEN a.first_sec
         ELSE LAG(a.last_sec) OVER wp END                               AS start_sec,
    -- how did it gain the ball?
    CASE
        WHEN a.first_type = 'Rebound' AND a.first_sub = 'Defensive'      THEN 'DREB'
        WHEN LAG(a.last_type) OVER wp = 'Made Shot'
          OR (LAG(a.last_type) OVER wp = 'Free Throw'
              AND LAG(a.last_result) OVER wp = 'Made')                  THEN 'MADE_BASKET'
        WHEN LAG(a.last_type) OVER wp = 'Turnover'
          AND LAG(a.last_sub) OVER wp IN ('Bad Pass', 'Lost Ball')       THEN 'LIVE_TOV'
        WHEN LAG(a.last_type) OVER wp = 'Turnover'                       THEN 'DEAD_BALL'
        ELSE 'UNKNOWN'
    END                                                                 AS start_type,

A possession changes whenever the ball-owning team changes between consecutive events, so offensive rebounds extend a possession. 03_windows.sql then flags the treatment: the team went 2-for-1 if its first shot decision (a field-goal attempt, turnover, or drawn shooting foul) came with 0:28 or more left. The outcome is team points minus opponent points from that moment to the buzzer.

The raw gap is +0.29 net points. But the two groups differ before anyone shoots: teams that went had the ball 4.7 seconds earlier on average and were 3.5 times as likely to start from a live-ball turnover. That is the confounding the models have to remove.

Result 1

A 2-for-1 is worth about a fifth of a point

Three estimators, from naive to causal. OLS adds controls for the clock, how the ball was gained, period, season, team offensive rating, opponent defensive rating, score margin and home court, with standard errors clustered by game. Inverse propensity weighting models each team's chance of going 2-for-1 and reweights so the two groups match on those same factors.

R/01_effect_models.R (excerpt)R
f_ols <- net_pts ~ went_2for1 + start_bucket + start_type + period + season +
                   team_off_rtg + opp_def_rtg + margin_c + is_home
m_ols <- lm(f_ols, data = w)
ct_ols <- coeftest(m_ols, vcov = vcovCL(m_ols, cluster = ~game_id))

f_ps <- went_2for1 ~ ns(start_sec, df = 4) + start_type + period + season +
                     team_off_rtg + opp_def_rtg + margin_c + is_home

ipw_ate <- function(d) {
  ps <- predict(glm(f_ps, family = binomial, data = d), type = "response")
  ps <- pmin(pmax(ps, 0.05), 0.95)                     # trim for overlap
  p1 <- mean(d$went_2for1)
  sw <- ifelse(d$went_2for1 == 1, p1 / ps, (1 - p1) / (1 - ps))  # stabilized
  weighted.mean(d$net_pts[d$went_2for1 == 1], sw[d$went_2for1 == 1]) -
    weighted.mean(d$net_pts[d$went_2for1 == 0], sw[d$went_2for1 == 0])
}

boot    <- replicate(500, {
  g <- sample(games, length(games), replace = TRUE)
  ipw_ate(w[unlist(idx[g], use.names = FALSE), ])
})

Checking the answer. Because the league is simulated, I replayed 6,000 sampled windows twenty times each with the decision forced both ways and everything else fixed. The true effect is +0.215 (SE 0.008). OLS lands within 0.01 of it and the IPW interval covers it. The naive estimate overstates it by 36%, and its interval only just reaches the true value. On real data there is no answer key, which is why the adjusted methods matter.

Result 2

It only pays if you gain the ball with 0:33 or more

Splitting the windows by when the team gained the ball, and fitting the adjusted model inside each 3-second bucket, shows where the value comes from. Below 0:33 a team has to shoot within about 3 seconds of getting the ball, and the estimate is indistinguishable from zero. From 0:33 to 0:39 the gain is about +0.3.

The late buckets are the hardest to measure: from 0:42 on, 93% of teams go, so there are few teams that didn't to compare against and the interval is wide. Every bucket's interval still covers its true value. Below 0:33 the true gain is +0.08, about a third of what it is with more time.

Result 3

Shoot between 0:34 and 0:28, not before

A team that gains the ball with 0:40 left and fires at 0:36 hands the opponent 35 seconds, which is enough for the opponent to run its own 2-for-1. Among attempts where the ball was gained with 0:37 or more, shooting at 0:34+ cost 0.20 net points after adjustment (SE 0.08).

Result 4

The ledger: points left on the table

This grades decisions, not results. Every pass (ball gained with 0:33+ and no 2-for-1) is charged the league-wide value of going, +0.258, and every too-early shot its cost, 0.201. A missed layup or a lucky heave doesn't move a team's grade. Points convert to wins at roughly 30 points of season margin per win.

Passing on the move is the bigger leak for almost every team; the three most conservative teams went 2-for-1 on only 41–50% of green-zone chances and leave 13–15 points a season. Limitations
  • The data is simulated, so the magnitudes are illustrative. The pipeline and the identification strategy are the product.
  • The treatment is defined by timing, so it mixes intent with execution: a quick transition layup counts as a 2-for-1.
  • Play-by-play doesn't show timeouts remaining, who is on the floor, or who has the ball in their hands.
  • Overlap is thin at 0:42–0:45, where almost every team goes.
